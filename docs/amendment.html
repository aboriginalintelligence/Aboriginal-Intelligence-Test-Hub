Membership Code

A written request signed by at least 25% of Electors (Appendix D) is delivered to Council. Council prepares and presents Document 08. Notice is posted for three months. Consent is given by a majority of all Electors on the list, by referendum with mail-in voting or by petition verified by an independent electoral officer.

3. Expedited procedure

Where Council passes all Resolutions at one meeting:

  • The steps for Day 0 in section 2 are taken at a single Council meeting.
  • The Special Meeting on Document 05 is held at least 14 days after notice is posted. The same notice may include the election of the Finance and Audit Committee, the Housing Allocation Committee, and the Appeals Panel Elector Members, because the Codes that create them are in force from Day 0.
  • Written request forms under section 13 of the Membership Code are made available at the Special Meeting, so that the three-month notice for Document 08 can start the same day.
  • The Transitional Election, the Fundamental Vote on Documents 03 and 04, and the Ratification Votes on the Codes are held on one voting day, each with its own ballot (Document 17 s. 5.3).
  • Where terms of office have expired, Document 05 section 28.6 requires a General Election for all eight Seats within 120 days. Section 28.5 permits notice periods for the first Election to be shortened by up to one third.

This procedure requires a Council able to pass Resolutions with a quorum present at a duly convened meeting.

4. Where Council does not act

A court does not order a Council to adopt a law. The following steps are available to Electors.

4.1 Election

Section 5.1 of the 2014 Regulations requires General Elections in June, and section 6.1 requires Council to set the date by Resolution at least 50 days before. The 2014 Regulations do not give Council power to postpone or cancel a General Election. An Elector may apply to the Federal Court for judicial review, including an order that the election be held. An application for judicial review is generally filed within 30 days after the decision is first communicated (Federal Courts Act, s. 18.1(2)).

4.2 Candidate commitments

Candidates may sign Document 01 or Document 19 before the election. Under the 2014 Regulations, a quorum of Council is two of three members, so two members elected on these commitments can pass every Resolution in section 2.

4.3 Steps started by Electors

  • A written request of 25% of Electors under section 13 of the Membership Code (Appendix D). Council is required to prepare and present the amendments.
  • A petition asking Council to take the steps in section 2 (Appendix E). The petition is not binding on Council.

4.4 Existing rights to information

  • Indian Act s. 86(2): a copy of any by-law on request.
  • By-law No. 2014.2 s. 4.1: an Annual General Assembly, quarterly Information Meetings, copies of the audit, and by-laws and major policies on request.
  • First Nations Financial Transparency Act ss. 7 and 10: copies of the audited consolidated financial statements and the schedule of remuneration and expenses, and an application to a superior court if they are not provided. Section 11: any person may apply if they are not published.
  • Requests are made in writing (Appendix F).

4.5 Indigenous Services Canada

Indigenous Services Canada, Alberta Region, may be asked in writing which Council members it has on record and the dates their terms end.

4.6 Removal under the 2014 Regulations

Section 18: a petition signed by 51% of all Electors, verified by Council; a Special Meeting within 14 days; and approval by at least 60% of the number of Electors who voted in the previous Election.

4.7 Limits

  • An amendment to the Election Regulations adopted other than under section 20 of the 2014 Regulations is open to challenge in court.
  • A vote of the Electors organized without a Resolution of Council may show the will of the community, but its legal effect is uncertain and should be reviewed by legal counsel first.
  • Under paragraph 3(1)(b) of the First Nations Elections Act, the Minister may add a First Nation to the schedule of that Act if satisfied that a protracted leadership dispute has significantly compromised its governance. Elections are then held under that Act.
  • A candidate commitment does not require an elected person to vote in a particular way.

5. Composition of Council

Legal requirement

Section 4.1 of the 2014 Regulations sets Council at one Chief and two Councillors. The number may be changed only by an amendment under section 20. Only persons elected under the Regulations are members of Council.

Reference

For bands that hold elections under the Indian Act, section 74(2) of that Act provides for one councillor for every one hundred members, with a minimum of two and a maximum of twelve. It does not apply to bands that select their council by custom.

7. Special Meeting checklist (2014 Regulations, section 20)

  • Obtain the number of Electors who voted in the previous Election. At least half of that number must be present (s. 20.7).
  • Post notice at least 14 days before the meeting at the band office and other designated locations, and mail it to Non-resident Electors who have provided an address, with the full amendments attached (ss. 20.3 to 20.5).
  • Provide registration, the List of Electors, and identification checks.
  • Appoint an independent chair and vote counter. Use sealed ballot boxes and count in public.
  • Record the figures required for the affidavit: the number who voted in the previous Election, the number present, the votes for and against, and the rejected ballots (s. 20.8).
  • Arrange transportation and accessibility for Non-resident Electors and Elders.

8. Fundamental Vote and Ratification Vote checklist (Document 17)

  • Appoint an independent electoral officer who meets the qualifications in section 8.3 of Document 05 (Document 17 s. 19.2).
  • Notice at least 45 days before a Fundamental Vote and at least 30 days before a Ratification Vote, with the full text and a plain-language summary approved by the electoral officer (s. 6).
  • Mail-in ballots for every Elector who requests one, and polling stations on Reserve 176A and in each city with at least 100 Electors (s. 8.1).
  • Information meetings on a Reserve and online at least 14 days before the vote (s. 6.4).
  • Count in public. Publish the number of Electors on the list, ballots cast, and votes for and against within three days, and certify whether the standard was met (s. 9).

10. Records required

  • The signed 2014 Regulations and the record of the meeting at which they were approved.
  • The Membership Code as in force, with any amendments.
  • The number of Electors who voted in the previous Election.
  • Any Resolution of Council concerning the date of the next General Election.
  • The names of the members of Council in office, their oath dates, and the dates their terms end.
  • Confirmation of whether By-law No. 2014.2 was published under section 86 of the Indian Act.
  • Existing finance, housing, education, personnel, and travel policies, signing authority Resolutions, and the list of bank and payment card accounts.
  • A list of loans to Members outstanding, contracts over $10,000 in force, and First Nation Business Entities with their directors.
  • Whether the First Nation is scheduled under the First Nations Fiscal Management Act.
